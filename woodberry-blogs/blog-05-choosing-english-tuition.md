# Choosing an English Tuition Class: 10 Questions Parents Should Ask

Most parents pick an English class the way they pick a restaurant — a neighbour recommended it, so they try it. Sometimes it works. Often it doesn't, and six months later the child's English hasn't moved and the term fee is gone.

Here are ten questions you can ask before you register. None of them are rude. A good class will answer all of them without hesitation. If a class refuses to answer even three, keep looking.

## 1. Who will teach my child?

Not "who owns the institute." Who stands in front of the batch. Get the name. Ask what classes they've taught before and how long they've been teaching this level. A good teacher is happy to tell you. An institute that dodges this is hiding something.

## 2. How many students are in a batch?

You want a number, not "small groups." For a speaking-focused class, 8 to 12 is the comfortable range. For exam prep, up to 20 is fine if written work is marked. Over 25 and the class is really a lecture.

## 3. Can I sit in on a class before I register?

Any serious institute will let you observe one class — sometimes from the back of the room, sometimes a sample session. If a class refuses, there's usually a reason, and it's usually not a good one.

## 4. What does a typical lesson look like?

Ask for a walk-through of one actual lesson, not the brochure version. "We open with a 10-minute vocabulary review, then one past paper comprehension passage, then 15 minutes of pair speaking, then a written homework check" is a specific answer. "We cover the syllabus" is not.

## 5. How is written work marked?

For any exam-prep class, hand-marked written work is non-negotiable. The marks should come back with corrections and short comments, not a tick and a grade. If the class uses only self-marking or peer-marking, your child isn't getting real feedback on their writing.

## 6. How will I know my child is improving?

Good answer: a written progress note at the end of each term, plus marked past papers you can see. Bad answer: "You can ask the teacher any time." You won't, and both of you know it.

## 7. What's the timetable, and how often does it change?

Classes that shift time every month are harder on families than they look. Ask for the full term's timetable in writing. Also check start and end times against your real evening — Matara traffic, school pickup, dinner.

## 8. What happens if my child misses a class?

Good classes offer either a makeup class, a recording, or notes with the week's work. If the answer is "they just miss it," you're paying for classes that disappear.

## 9. What materials are included in the fee?

Ask if books, past paper packs, and mock tests are inside the term fee or charged separately. This matters especially for IELTS and A/L classes, where materials can add 20–30% to the real cost.

## 10. Who are last year's results?

For exam-focused classes, ask about recent student results — not individual student names, but overall patterns. "Most of our O/L students passed with Credit" is a fair answer. "100% distinctions" is almost never true.

## The three warning signs

If you hear any of these, walk away.

- **Guaranteed grades.** No honest teacher promises a specific O/L grade or IELTS band. Learning depends on the student too.
- **Full-year fee upfront with no refund terms.** A term at a time is reasonable. Twelve months with no exit is a trap.
- **No teachers' qualifications shared.** You don't need degrees in English — some excellent teachers don't have them — but the institute should be willing to tell you about them.

## The three green flags

- The office staff know your child's name by the second visit.
- The teacher knows the Department of Examinations marking scheme by heart.
- A former student recommends the class without being asked.

## Big-brand vs local vs online

### Big brand (British Council, Royal Institute, major Colombo names)

Strong curriculum, good materials, higher fees. For IELTS especially, the brand is useful because the materials are current. Downside: large batch sizes, less personal attention, often a commute.

### Local institute

The real variable. The good ones beat big brands easily because the teacher-to-student ratio is better and the owner is in the building. The weak ones have one tired teacher doing six batches. The questions above sort the two.

### Online

Convenient for adults and older students. Weak for primary-age children unless a parent sits in. Good for exam prep if the class uses live writing feedback and not just recorded videos.

## When to switch classes

A few signs it's time to move:

- Six months in and your child can't name one thing they learned last week.
- The teacher changes four times a year.
- Written work comes back with only a tick, no corrections.
- Your child dreads the class — not in a "homework is hard" way, but a "nothing's happening there" way.

Switching mid-year isn't ideal, but staying in a class that isn't working is worse.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should I expect to pay for English tuition in Sri Lanka?**

Fees vary widely by city, level, and class size. Group classes for school students usually sit in a lower bracket per hour than private tuition; IELTS and A/L main English cost more because of smaller batches and materials. If a class is dramatically cheaper than others nearby, ask why — usually it's batch size.

**Is private tuition always better than group class?**

No. Group classes work better for speaking because speaking needs other speakers. Private tuition works better when a child is far ahead or far behind the group, or for a specific exam on a short runway.

**Should I pick the nearest English class or the best one?**

Within reason, the nearest one, if the quality is close. Traffic and tiredness kill classes that start too late at night. A slightly weaker class your child attends every week beats a slightly better class they skip twice a month.

**Can I move my child mid-term if the class isn't working?**

Yes. Most institutes won't refund the full term fee, but a wasted term is cheaper than a wasted year. If the warning signs are clear, move.
