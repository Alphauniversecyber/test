# IELTS vs TOEFL in Sri Lanka: Which One Should You Take?

If you're applying overseas from Sri Lanka, someone has probably already told you to take IELTS. Someone else told you TOEFL is harder. A third person said "it doesn't matter, just take one." All three are partly right.

Here's a straight comparison, written for Sri Lankan candidates, with the local practicalities in mind.

## The short version

- **IELTS** is more common in Sri Lanka, has in-person speaking with a real examiner, and is accepted almost everywhere.
- **TOEFL** is more common for US universities, uses a computer-based speaking section, and is accepted in most places too.
- **For most Sri Lankan candidates, IELTS is the easier logistical choice.** TOEFL still wins for some US applications.

If you need a specific answer now: ask your target university or visa route. Pick the test they explicitly accept. If both work, take IELTS.

## Format differences

### IELTS Academic

- **Listening:** 30 minutes, four recorded sections.
- **Reading:** 60 minutes, three passages.
- **Writing:** 60 minutes, a short data task plus a longer essay.
- **Speaking:** 11–14 minutes, face to face with an examiner.
- Total: about 2 hours 45 minutes.

### TOEFL iBT

- **Reading:** 35 minutes.
- **Listening:** 36 minutes.
- **Speaking:** 16 minutes, recorded into a microphone, graded later.
- **Writing:** 29 minutes, two tasks.
- Total: about 2 hours.

Both tests cover the same four skills, but the way they test them is different. TOEFL is fully computer-based and has shorter sections. IELTS can be done on paper or computer depending on the centre, with speaking always done live with a person.

## Speaking: the biggest practical difference

If you're comfortable with people and nervous with microphones, IELTS speaking will feel more natural. You sit across from an examiner, they ask questions, you answer. It's a conversation.

TOEFL speaking is harder for most Sri Lankan candidates. You speak into a microphone against a timer, often with other test takers speaking in the same room. There's no interaction. If you pause too long or go off-topic, no one guides you back.

Candidates who speak well with people but freeze with recording equipment usually do better on IELTS.

## Scoring

**IELTS:** scored on a 9-band scale, with 0.5 increments. Band 7 is competitive. Most universities ask for 6.0 to 7.5 depending on programme.

**TOEFL iBT:** scored out of 120, with 30 per section. 90 is roughly equivalent to IELTS 7. Most universities ask for 80–100.

Score requirements for Sri Lankan universities and overseas universities vary by programme — always check the specific requirement on the course page rather than trusting general numbers.

## Accepted where?

**Both tests** are accepted by universities in the UK, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Ireland, and most European countries.

**IELTS** has an edge for:

- UK visa and immigration (IELTS for UKVI is the standard route).
- Australian permanent residency.
- UK and Irish universities.
- Most Sri Lankan employers and government scholarships.

**TOEFL** has an edge for:

- Many US universities, especially older ones.
- A smaller number of institutions that only accept TOEFL.

If your target is clear, check the university or visa route's accepted tests page. If your target is vague, IELTS covers more ground.

## Cost and availability in Sri Lanka

Both tests cost more than most people expect. IELTS and TOEFL fees are in a similar range, with slight variation by test type and date. Check current fees on IDP Sri Lanka, British Council, or the ETS TOEFL site — prices change.

**Availability in Sri Lanka:**

- IELTS test dates are frequent, both in Colombo and occasionally at other centres.
- TOEFL iBT is also offered in Colombo; test slots are less frequent than IELTS.

If you need to test soon, IELTS almost always has a nearer date.

## Which is easier?

Honestly, neither is "harder." They're different. What matters is which plays to your strengths.

**IELTS tends to feel easier for:**

- Candidates with British-accent exposure (British Council classes, UK TV, UK English textbooks).
- People who prefer face-to-face speaking.
- Candidates who write faster by hand than on a keyboard.

**TOEFL tends to feel easier for:**

- Candidates with American-accent exposure.
- Fast typists comfortable with a keyboard.
- People who prefer no one watching them speak.

Most Sri Lankan students have grown up with British-leaning English through schooling. That tilts things toward IELTS without most candidates realising it.

## Practice material

**IELTS** has the Cambridge IELTS test books (1 through 18). These are the gold standard. Every serious candidate should work through at least three of them.

**TOEFL** has the Official Guide to the TOEFL iBT and the official practice tests from ETS. Fewer high-quality third-party books than IELTS has, but plenty of practice through the ETS website.

Free material exists for both tests. Paid official material is still worth the money because it's closest to the real exam.

## Preparation time

The time commitment is roughly the same for either test if you're starting at the same level. A candidate at intermediate English aiming for IELTS 7 or TOEFL 90 usually needs 6 to 10 weeks of focused preparation.

## Our honest recommendation

For most Sri Lankan candidates:

- **Take IELTS** unless your target university or visa route specifically prefers TOEFL.
- If you're applying to a mix of US and non-US universities, check whether all of them accept IELTS. Usually they do.
- If you're applying to one specific US university that prefers TOEFL, take TOEFL.
- Don't take both unless you've failed to reach the required score on one and your test date is approaching. It's a waste of money.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is IELTS or TOEFL cheaper in Sri Lanka?**

The two tests cost similar amounts, with small differences depending on test type and date. Check current fees on IDP, British Council, and ETS websites before booking.

**Can I use IELTS for a US university application?**

Most US universities accept IELTS now, but a small number still prefer TOEFL. Check the accepted tests page of each university you're applying to before deciding.

**How long are IELTS and TOEFL scores valid?**

Both are valid for two years from the test date. Plan your test timing around when you'll actually submit applications.

**Should I take the paper-based or computer-based IELTS?**

If you write faster by hand, take paper. If you type faster, take computer. Computer-based IELTS usually has results returned faster and more flexible dates.
