# How to Prepare for Grade 5 Scholarship in Six Months

The Grade 5 Scholarship Examination lands with more weight than any other paper a Sri Lankan child will sit until A/Ls. Every parent we meet wants the same thing: a clear plan that doesn't break their child. Here's one.

Six months is enough time if you start now and keep going. Less than three months, and you move to a different plan entirely (focus on past papers and sleep). This guide assumes you've got the full six months.

## Month 0: the week before you start

Before any studying, do three things.

- Sit down with your child and explain what the exam is, simply. Not scary. Just honest. "There's a paper in [month]. We'll do a little practice each week. You don't have to score the highest in Sri Lanka. You have to do your best."
- Buy or download one recent past paper. Let your child try it without any pressure. Don't mark it. Just watch.
- Decide the weekly study slot. One hour on a weekday, one longer session on the weekend. Protect it from everything else.

## Month 1: foundations

**Goal:** the child understands the shape of the paper and starts building habits.

- **Daily:** 20 minutes of reading in both Sinhala/Tamil and English. Storybooks, newspapers for children, anything with sentences.
- **Weekly:** one practice paper section (not a full paper yet). Mark it together.
- **Vocabulary:** 10 new English words a week. Make flashcards with the word on one side and a picture on the other. Keep them.
- **No timing yet.** Speed comes later. Right now you're building confidence.

Common parent mistake in Month 1: hitting the past papers too hard, too early. The child burns out before February.

## Month 2: question types

**Goal:** the child recognises every type of question that shows up.

- **General knowledge:** work through the categories that appear most often — our country (geography, famous landmarks), environment (plants, animals, weather), our history (quick facts, not deep dives), basic science.
- **Intelligence section:** number patterns, shape patterns, odd one out. Short daily puzzles beat long weekly sessions.
- **English:** continue the 10 words a week. Add preposition practice — *beside, between, under, over, through*. Picture-matching worksheets help a lot here.
- **One full practice paper this month.** Untimed. Mark together.

## Month 3: the midpoint check

**Goal:** honest assessment of where the child is.

Do one past paper under full timing. Mark it with the official marking scheme. Now look at the result honestly:

- Which section is weakest? General knowledge, intelligence, or English?
- Which question types keep going wrong?
- Is the child running out of time, or running out of answers?

Spend the second half of Month 3 fixing the weakest area specifically. Don't try to fix everything at once.

## Month 4: building speed

**Goal:** the child can finish full papers within the time limit.

- **Weekly:** one past paper under full timing. Same time of day you'd sit the real paper.
- **Daily:** 15 minutes of mental maths and English vocabulary drill — fast, short bursts.
- **Weekly marking:** sit together, go through every wrong answer. Ask *why* your child picked that option. The answer usually tells you what to teach.

By the end of Month 4, your child should be finishing the paper within the time with a few minutes to check.

## Month 5: question-type drilling

**Goal:** turn remaining weak spots into automatic answers.

This is where tutoring, class, or a structured programme helps if you haven't had one so far. A good class has seen every question type a hundred times and can spot the exact pattern your child is missing.

- Two past papers a week, alternating full and half.
- Focused drill on the two or three question types that still cost marks.
- Light on new content. You're not adding, you're sharpening.

## Month 6: taper and calm

**Goal:** the child walks into the exam hall rested and confident.

- Reduce practice volume in the last two weeks.
- Full timed papers on the weekends only.
- No new topics — if your child doesn't know it by now, it's not coming in the exam.
- Sleep normally. Early nights for the last week.
- Day before the exam: a light review, a favourite meal, early bed.

## The English component, specifically

Inside the general paper, the English questions are worth real marks. Don't neglect them.

What shows up most:

- Picture-to-word matching
- Short reading passages with multiple-choice questions
- Preposition and position words (between, beside, under, over)
- Simple grammar (is/are, was/were, do/does)
- Basic question words (what, where, when, who, why, how)

What doesn't show up:

- Essay writing
- Complex tenses
- Long grammar explanations

Spend English preparation time on what actually appears. Vocabulary drilling and short comprehension passages are your best use of time. See our [full guide to the Grade 5 Scholarship English section](https://woodberryplace.com/blog/grade-5-scholarship-english-paper/) for a deeper breakdown.

## How much a child should actually study

The honest numbers:

- **Weekdays:** 30 to 45 minutes.
- **Saturdays:** 1.5 to 2 hours (one practice paper + marking).
- **Sundays:** off, or light review.

Total: around 5–6 hours a week. Enough to make steady progress. More than that, and you'll see tired, resistant, less effective sessions. A child who hates studying will not score higher than a child who doesn't.

## What parents often get wrong

- **Treating the child's results as their own.** Your child will have a bad practice paper. It's a practice paper. Breathe.
- **Comparing with other children.** Every parent group has one parent whose child is "already doing papers from last year." Mute the group.
- **Last-minute panic in Month 6.** Doing double the work in the last four weeks undoes four months of calm prep.
- **Praising only the score.** Praise effort, patience, the question types they got right. The score is a result, not the goal.

## When things aren't working

If three months in, your child is losing ground or hating the process:

- Reduce the load, don't raise it.
- Switch to a shorter daily routine with more variety.
- Join a class if you've been doing this alone. A good small-group class takes some of the weight off parents and children both.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should my child start preparing for the Grade 5 Scholarship?**

Six months out is comfortable. One full year gives more room. Starting earlier than that usually tires the child before the exam.

**How many past papers should my child do before the exam?**

Around 15–20 past papers over six months, mixed between timed and untimed. Doing more than that usually means you're repeating patterns rather than learning new ones.

**What should I do if my child says they don't want to do the scholarship?**

Listen. Sometimes the answer is that the pressure around them is too high; sometimes they're tired; sometimes they're not confident. Backing off for a week often restarts the momentum. Forcing it rarely helps.

**Do all children need tuition for the Grade 5 Scholarship?**

No. Children who read a lot at home, have patient parents, and work through past papers can do very well without a formal class. A class helps most when parents don't have the time, or the child needs a group setting to focus.
