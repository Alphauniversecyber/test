# English Classes vs School English: What Private Tuition Actually Adds

Not every child needs English tuition. Some children get strong English at home, at school, through reading, and never see the inside of a tuition class. If that's your family, good — save the money and the evenings.

But many parents sign up for English tuition out of a vague feeling that it's necessary, without knowing what the class is actually supposed to add. Here's a clearer way to think about it.

## What school English does well

Sri Lankan schools, both government and private, cover a lot of English well:

- **Grammar foundations.** Tenses, parts of speech, sentence structure.
- **Reading comprehension.** Texts at age-appropriate levels, standard question types.
- **Writing basics.** Letter writing, essays, short compositions.
- **Vocabulary.** Through readers, textbooks, and classroom discussion.
- **Exam format familiarity.** Students see past paper questions from the O/L and A/L syllabus through the year.

If your child has a good English teacher and a reasonable class size, school English covers the written side of the subject reasonably well.

## What school English struggles with

Class sizes of 35 to 45 children mean three things get crowded out, whatever the teacher's skill:

### 1. Speaking practice

Thirty-five children, forty minutes, maybe one student speaks per minute for a few seconds. By the end of a year, each child has spoken English in class for a total of maybe five hours. That's not enough to build fluency.

### 2. Individual feedback on writing

A teacher with 45 essays to mark can give ticks and grades, not detailed feedback. Students don't know exactly what pushed them from a B to a C, or how to get to an A.

### 3. Specific weakness coaching

A child who struggles with articles, or reported speech, or essay structure can't get individual attention in a large class. The teacher has to move on.

These three gaps are what tuition classes exist to fill. Any English class that doesn't actively fix at least one of them isn't really adding anything.

## What a good English tuition class should add

If you're considering tuition, this is the measure. A class is worth it when it adds one or more of these:

### Real speaking time

Small groups where every student speaks every lesson. Not big "spoken English" classes where speaking is a theoretical topic. If your child doesn't come home more comfortable speaking English after three months, the class isn't adding speaking.

### Hand-marked writing

Essays and letters returned with corrections, specific comments, and suggestions. Not a tick and a grade. If the only mark on the page is a tick, writing isn't improving.

### Exam-specific coaching

Past paper drills, marking scheme familiarity, specific question-type weaknesses addressed. Especially important in Grades 10, 11, and A/L.

### Fluency through small groups

Peer learning. Children speak more freely around other children than around adults, and more freely still in groups of eight than in classes of forty.

### Confidence

A child who's shy in school because the class is huge may be the loudest in a small tuition group. That's real progress.

## What a bad English tuition class adds

Be honest with yourself about what you're paying for. Tuition isn't worth it if:

- The class is just school material covered again, in a different room.
- The batch has 30 students (same as school).
- Written work is returned with ticks only.
- Your child says nothing in class.
- The teacher reads from a book for an hour.

In this case, you're paying for a second school day. Your child would do better with more sleep.

## When tuition genuinely helps

Some clear scenarios where tuition adds real value:

### For speaking

A child reads English fluently but freezes when asked to speak. School can't fix this. A small-group spoken English class can.

### For exam prep

Grades 10 and 11 students facing O/L, and A/L students in their final year. Past-paper drilling and marking-scheme familiarity are where tuition pays off most clearly.

### For foundations if school was weak

Children who had weak primary-school English — maybe the teacher was off sick a lot, or the school's English programme is poor — benefit hugely from a solid tuition class to catch up.

### For specific tests

IELTS, TOEFL, specific overseas or scholarship tests. School doesn't teach these. Tuition does.

### For working adults

School English is long behind you. A class is often the quickest way back to usable English.

## When tuition probably isn't needed

A few honest scenarios:

- Your child reads English books for fun. (A reader at home will often outpace a student in class.)
- Your child speaks English at home with at least one parent or sibling.
- Your child is in Grades 1–3 and school English is going fine. More English isn't automatically better — childhood matters.
- You can't actually afford it. A stressed family is worse than a slightly weaker English grade. There are free alternatives — library books, YouTube, English cartoons, conversation practice at home.

## How to pick, if you decide you want tuition

Four questions to ask before you register:

- **Will my child actually speak in class?** Ask the question literally. Get a real answer.
- **How is written work marked?** Ticks only = don't bother. Corrections and comments = good.
- **What's the batch size?** Under 15 is good. Over 25 is just school again.
- **Can I observe one class?** Any good class will let you watch.

## How to get the most out of tuition if you commit

- **Keep the slot sacred.** Tuition skipped half the time is tuition wasted. Pick a day and time you can keep.
- **Follow up at home.** Ten minutes of English at home each day multiplies the class effect.
- **Talk to the teacher.** Once a term. Ask what your child is weakest at. Fix at home what's not being covered in class.
- **Don't double up classes.** Two English tuitions in a week is usually one too many. One strong class plus home practice beats two weak classes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is private English tuition necessary in Sri Lanka?**

No — not for every child. It's genuinely useful when school is leaving gaps (speaking, marked writing, exam-specific coaching) and it's not useful when it just doubles up on what school already covers.

**When should I start English tuition for my child?**

Usually from around Grade 3 or 4, if your child needs it. Earlier can work if the class is play-based. Starting tuition before age 5 is rarely necessary.

**How many classes a week does my child need?**

One well-chosen class a week, plus some English at home, is enough for most students. Twice-weekly is appropriate for exam-year students or children catching up on a weak foundation.

**Can tuition replace school English?**

No. Tuition supplements school English. The school's classroom time, syllabus coverage, and peer group all matter. A good tuition class adds the parts the school can't deliver with 40 students in a room.
